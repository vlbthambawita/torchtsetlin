\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:method}

The library already computes the full $(B, P, C)$ patch-match tensor inside
\code{\_ConvMixin.\_evaluate} and discards its layout --- it returns
\code{matches.any(dim=1)} and passes the rest on only as an opaque feedback context. The
whole \mctm primitive is therefore a reshape, implemented in
\code{experiments/mctm/mctm.py}:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\ttoprule
\thead{Function} & \thead{Role} \\
\ttmidrule
\code{conv\_clause\_maps} & Reshapes $(B, P, C) \to (B, C, P_y, P_x)$. Forces
  \code{empty\_value=False} so a clause with no included literals never emits an all-ones
  channel --- every clause is empty at initialisation, and a constant-$1$ channel is exactly
  what a downstream conjunction latches onto. \\
\code{or\_pool} & $k\times k$ OR pooling (max-pool on $0/1$ bits). Its role here is
  \emph{density control}: a $2\times2$ OR lifts a $2\%$ firing rate to $\approx 8\%$. \\
\code{randomize\_clauses} & The random layer-1 control: \code{n\_include} literals per
  clause, each a random feature with a random sign, never both $x$ and $\neg x$ (which would
  make the clause unsatisfiable). \\
\code{MCTM} & The frozen stack plus head; \code{transform} streams, the driver materialises. \\
\code{firing\_stats} & Per-channel firing rate, dead and saturated fractions. \\
\ttbotrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Because a frozen layer's output is constant across epochs, the driver materialises it once
rather than recomputing per batch.

\subsection{Deviations from the original pseudocode}

The proposal document specifies a few details that do not survive contact with the
algorithm; the implementation departs from it as follows.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \lead{Type~Ib needs no patch.} The pseudocode draws \code{selected\_patch} from
  \code{matching\_positions} before branching on feedback type, but a clause that matched
  nowhere still receives Type~I feedback and has no matching patch to draw. Type~Ib is
  patch-free.
\item \lead{One patch draw per feedback \emph{event}.} A shared (coalesced) clause can
  receive Type~Ia and Type~II in the same update, and each must draw its own patch. The
  library already does this in \code{\_feedback\_counts}.
\item \lead{Thermometer, not binary, position codes.} The pseudocode uses
  \code{BINARY\_ENCODE(row)}. Base-2 codes are not monotone, so no conjunction can express
  ``row in $[a,b]$'', and rows 7 and 8 look maximally different. Thermometer bits
  $[y > k]$ make a region constraint two literals wide.
\item \lead{Empty clauses are False in a feature layer}, always, regardless of training mode.
\end{itemize}
