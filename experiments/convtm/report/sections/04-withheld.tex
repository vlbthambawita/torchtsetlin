\section{Withheld: the capacity ladder}
\label{sec:withheld}

The decisive experiment of the reproduction phase asked whether accuracy keeps rising with clause
count. The literature says it does: the same encoding gains \textbf{+\LadderRisePub} points from
20\,000 to 80\,000 total clauses and is still climbing at its largest measured cell. We measure it
flat.

The 20\,000 point reproduces well. The curve then fails to rise: \textbf{+\LadderRise\ points on the
plateau where the literature reports +\LadderRisePub}.

\textbf{This is reported as withheld, not as a result.} Two explanations are live and one seed cannot
separate them:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the published capacity rise does not reproduce; or
\item \textbf{50 epochs is too few at the top end}. The source ran 250. Our epoch correction was
      digitised from a figure at \emph{one} clause count and may not transfer to a model four times
      larger. The convergence check is ambiguous: the 80\,000 arm's last ten validation epochs read
      63.0, 63.4, 63.0, 64.9, 63.9, 62.8, 64.9, 64.8, 63.3, 63.3 --- neither converged nor clearly
      climbing.
\end{enumerate}

If (1) survives a second seed and a longer epoch budget it is a consequential negative result about a
published table, and it would need stating very carefully. If (2) holds, the ladder simply needed more
compute. The programme was halted before either could be run.

\subsection{A defect in our own method, found by the team}
While reading the ladder, the selection rule itself failed inspection. \code{selected\_epoch =
argmax(val\_acc)} over 50 epochs is a \textbf{biased estimator on a noisy curve}. Measured across the
three ladder arms: plateau standard deviation \textbf{0.80--0.94} points; binomial floor on 5\,000
validation images at $p\approx0.65$ is \textbf{0.67} points; and \code{argmax} inflates the reported
validation figure by \textbf{+1.62, +1.65, +1.67} points --- a consistency across three independent
arms that is the signature of a pure order-statistic effect.

The consequence is that a decision rule with a \SeedBand\ point threshold was being fed an estimator
carrying about 1.65 points of selection noise, and the test accuracy at the winning epoch is an
essentially uncorrelated draw from the plateau rather than the best model. The 80\,000 arm's selected
epoch 16 is a noise spike; epochs 44--48 sit at the same level.

This is not leakage --- selection remained validation-only throughout --- but it is a precision
defect, and it is the reason the ladder verdict is withheld rather than merely under-seeded. The
repair costs \textbf{no GPU time}: every record stores its full per-epoch curve, so every existing arm
can be re-scored under a smoothed selection rule without a single re-run.
