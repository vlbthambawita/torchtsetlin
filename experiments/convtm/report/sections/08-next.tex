\section{What would settle the open questions}

In cost order, on the two GPUs used here.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Re-score every record under a smoothed selection rule.} No GPU time. Repairs the defect
      of \S\ref{sec:withheld} from stored curves and tightens every comparison in the programme.
      Must be done before anything else is concluded.
\item \textbf{Finish the accuracy decomposition} --- the Booleanized-input CNN and the binarised CNN,
      roughly 18 GPU-hours. These are the two controls that turn ``Tsetlin machines trail CNNs by 24
      points'' into a statement about \emph{which} component is responsible, and neither exists in the
      literature.
\item \textbf{Settle the capacity ladder}: a second seed and a longer-epoch run at 80\,000 clauses,
      roughly 15 GPU-hours. This decides whether a published table reproduces.
\item \textbf{The bloat-versus-headroom discriminator}, 0.6 GPU-hours: run the clause-budget pair on
      CIFAR-2 built from this pipeline. It separates ``the effect tracks how bloated the baseline is''
      from ``the effect tracks how much headroom the task has'', which is the difference between a
      general claim and a CIFAR-10 one.
\item \textbf{The optimum budget and what governs clause length}, roughly 5 GPU-hours. The budget
      sweep was prepared and not run; and the falsification of the admission-threshold account leaves
      the governing quantity unidentified, which is the most interesting open theoretical question the
      programme produced.
\item \textbf{The diversity-versus-ensembling control}, 3 GPU-hours. Twenty-two small specialists beat
      one large model by 4.1 points at 0.69$\times$ the automata; whether that is \emph{encoding
      diversity} or merely \emph{ensembling} has never been tested, and it is the cheapest route to a
      contribution this programme identified.
\end{enumerate}

Only after (1)--(3) would candidate generation for a new mechanism be well-founded. The team's own
pre-registered reading --- untested --- is that the gap is predominantly architectural rather than a
property of the learning rule, which would direct candidate generation at hierarchy, pooling that
preserves count information, and receptive-field growth, and away from modifications of the feedback
rule.
