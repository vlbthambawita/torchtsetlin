A five-member agentic research team was asked to find the best way to handle convolution in Tsetlin
machines and to close the gap to convolutional neural networks on CIFAR-10. It was halted after three
days, part-way through its reproduction phase. This report states what was established, what was
withheld, and what remains open.

Three results are solid. First, the number the field's CIFAR-10 progress is usually anchored to ---
\textbf{60.7\%} --- \textbf{is not a CIFAR-10 result}; it traces to an MSc thesis on a \emph{modified}
dataset, cited at third hand. The real published single-model bar is \textbf{75.4\%}. Second, a
\textbf{clause-size budget} --- a published mechanism whose own authors measured it at \textbf{+0.06}
points on CIFAR-2 and presented as an interpretability and hardware device --- is worth
\textbf{+\BudDelta\ points} on convolutional CIFAR-10 at 4.8$\times$ fewer literals per image. It
survives the exact sequential algorithm (+\SeqDelta) and it is \emph{not} substitutable by the
specificity parameter $s$. Third, the mechanism behind it is measured: literal inclusion is a
\textbf{ratchet} whose admission rule never asks whether a literal is useful, so clauses accumulate
logically redundant literals until they memorise patches rather than describe patterns.

Two central measurements are \textbf{withheld}. The capacity ladder is flat where the literature
reports a +\LadderRisePub\ point rise over the same range, but at one seed and one fifth of the
published epoch budget it cannot distinguish a reproduction failure from an under-trained arm. And
the programme's own validation-selection rule was found to be a biased estimator carrying more noise
than the decision threshold it feeds --- a defect in our method, found by the team, affecting every
arm, and fixable from stored data without a single re-run.
