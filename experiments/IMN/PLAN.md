## 1. What IMN is (from the paper and code)

A TabResNet hypernetwork `w(x; θ)` produces, for every example, the parameters of a linear model in the
original feature space. The logit is that linear model evaluated at the example:
`z_k(x) = Σ_m w_km(x)·x_m + w_k0(x)`. Training is end-to-end with
`CE + λ·mean|w(x)|`. The explanation of `x` is `w_m(x)·x_m` (paper Eq. 2).
Paper limitation (§7): "the per-instance models are linear".

Official defaults (from `main_experiment.py`, `models/model.py`, `utils.py`):
- Architecture: 2 residual blocks of width 128, GELU, dropout 0.25, zero-init last BN of each block.
- Optimiser: Adam with lr 0.01 and weight decay 0.01, 5 warm-up epochs, then cosine annealing with
  warm restarts (`T_mult = 2`, 3 restarts).
- Ensemble: snapshots at `T_0, 3T_0, 7T_0`.
- Training: L1 weight 0.1, 500 epochs, batch 64, augmentation off, one logit with BCE for binary tasks.

All of this is reproduced in `code/models.py` and `code/train_nn.py`.

## 2. Feasibility: what does "replace the linear model with a TM" mean?

The TM is a **sum of weighted conjunctions** of Boolean literals: `v_k(x) = Σ_j W_kj · c_j(x)`.
The obstacle is that clause learning is discrete. A clause's literal set has no gradient, so a hypernetwork cannot
emit clauses by back-propagation. The weights `W` on the clauses are continuous, though, and the
clause outputs `c_j(x) ∈ {0,1}` are fixed Boolean features of `x` once the clauses are frozen.
That gives four constructions:

| # | Construction | Feasible? | Arm |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Hypernetwork generates the **clause weights** of a frozen, pre-trained TM. Local model: `z_k(x) = b_k(x) + Σ_j W_kj(x)·c_j(x)`. | **Yes.** It is fully differentiable in `θ`, and the clauses come from ordinary TM training. This is the literal replacement: the local model *is* a (weighted) TM. | `imn_tm` |
| B | Local model = linear + clause terms. | **Yes.** It is the same as A plus IMN's linear term. | `imn_hybrid` |
| C | Swap the roles: a **TM is the hypernetwork** and the local model stays linear, `w_k(x) = w0_k + Σ_j c_j(x)·V_jk`. | **Yes.** It is linear in its parameters given the frozen clauses. There is no black box left: every coefficient of every local model can be traced to the rules that fired. | `tm_gated` |
| D | Hypernetwork generates the **clauses themselves** (literal include masks) per example. | **Not in this programme.** Masks are discrete and would need straight-through or Gumbel relaxations, so no TM learning would be involved. A rule generated *for* `x` is also trivially true or false at `x`, so the explanation degenerates unless it is constrained to hold on a neighbourhood. This is listed under future work (§11). | none |

A, B and C are implemented. They all share one practical constraint: **the clauses are learned
first, by TM feedback, and then frozen** (two-stage training). Joint training is listed under future work.

Related programme: [`experiments/linearModel`](../linearModel/PLAN.md) studies a construction close to
C on MNIST, with integer-state coordinate search. Here C uses continuous weights trained with Adam on
tabular data, under the IMN protocol. The two programmes share no code.

### 2.1 Why this might help, and why it might not

The arguments in this section are hypotheses (C5).

- Arguments for:
  - (i) A TM's explanation is a set of readable rules with thresholds ("`age ≥ 41 AND NOT
    capital-gain ≥ 0`") instead of a coefficient vector. IMN's own limitation section asks for
    non-linear local models of exactly this kind.
  - (ii) Clause outputs are constant within axis-aligned boxes. A local clause model can therefore be
    valid on a whole region instead of a tangent plane. This is measurable as local fidelity (§7).
  - (iii) A TM's fixed votes are a strong prior, and per-example re-weighting could fix the TM's
    weakest point: one global vote per clause.
- Arguments against:
  - (i) A hypernetwork that outputs `K·n_out` weights per example has far more freedom than IMN's
    `(d+1)·n_out`, and the credit-g pilot already shows over-fitting (`imn_tm` train AUROC 1.00, see
    DECISIONS D15).
  - (ii) A frozen clause basis caps what the local model can express. If the TM missed a pattern, no
    re-weighting recovers it, whereas IMN's linear term is always available.
  - (iii) Explanations over hundreds of active clauses may be *less* readable than `d` coefficients.
    This is measured as explanation size (§7).

## 3. Arms

Every arm sees the same splits, the same preprocessing, and the same seed. The TM-based arms share
**one cached source TM** per (dataset, seed, TM config): they use identical clauses.

| Arm | What it is | Interpretable by design? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lr` | Logistic regression (sklearn defaults, max_iter 1000) | yes (global) | paper baseline |
| `dt` | Decision tree (sklearn defaults) | yes (global) | paper baseline |
| `rf` | Random forest (500 trees) | no | paper baseline |
| `gbdt` | CatBoost if installed, else sklearn HistGradientBoosting (D8) | no | paper baseline |
| `tabresnet` | Same backbone as IMN with a linear classification head | no | paper baseline |
| `imn` | **IMN with a linear local model** (reproduction) | local linear | paper |
| `tm` | **Original TM** (weighted, thermometer Booleanization) | yes (global rules) | torchtsetlin |
| `clause_lr` | TM clauses with one *global* weight vector re-fit by gradient descent, starting from the TM's votes | yes (global rules) | control |
| `imn_tm` | **IMN with a TM local model** (construction A), initialised at the TM | local rules | new |
| `imn_tm_rand` | `imn_tm` on random data-anchored conjunctions with matched lengths | local rules | control |
| `imn_hybrid` | IMN with a linear + TM local model (construction B) | local linear + rules | new |
| `tm_gated` | TM-as-hypernetwork with a linear local model (construction C) | fully (no black box) | new |

The source TM:
- Inputs: `TabularBooleanizer` with up to 10 unique quantile thresholds per standardized column. A binary
  or low-cardinality column gets the cut midpoints instead.
- Model: `tt.TsetlinMachine(weighted=True)`, 200 clauses per class, `T = 0.5·n_clauses`, `s = 5`,
  batch feedback with batch 32.
- Training: up to 60 epochs, keeping the validation-AUROC-best epoch (patience 15).
- Clause basis: empty clauses dropped and duplicates merged (D13).

## 4. Questions

The pairwise tests are preregistered in `report.py::PAIRS`. Their unit is the dataset-level mean test
AUROC over seeds. Each pair gets a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Holm-corrected over all pairs. In
addition, the report shows average ranks, a Friedman test and the Nemenyi critical difference, as in the paper.

| ID | Question | Comparison | Success criterion (preregistered) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Does per-example re-weighting improve a TM? | `imn_tm` vs `tm` | Holm p < 0.05 and median Δ > 0 |
| Q2 | Does a TM local model cost accuracy relative to IMN's linear one? | `imn_tm` vs `imn` | **Non-inferiority:** mean Δ ≥ −0.01 AUROC with the upper end of a paired bootstrap 95 % CI of −Δ < 0.01 |
| Q3 | Is linear + rules better than either? | `imn_hybrid` vs `imn`, vs `imn_tm` | Holm p < 0.05, median Δ > 0 |
| Q4 | What does a fully interpretable generator (no black box) cost or gain? | `tm_gated` vs `lr`, `tm`, `imn` | Superior to `lr` and `tm` (Holm p < 0.05). The gap to `imn` is reported as the price of removing the black box. |
| Q5 | Is the *per-example* generator needed, or is a re-fit global clause weighting enough? | `imn_tm` vs `clause_lr` | Holm p < 0.05, median Δ > 0 |
| Q6 | Do *learned* clauses matter? | `imn_tm` vs `imn_tm_rand` | Holm p < 0.05, median Δ > 0 |
| Q7 | Are TM-based local explanations better? | §7 metrics, `imn_tm`/`imn_hybrid`/`tm_gated` vs `imn` | Reported descriptively. On the synthetic tasks, ground-truth correlation is the headline metric. |
| R | Reproduction check | `imn` vs `tabresnet`, vs paper Table 8 | `imn` not significantly worse than `tabresnet`, as in the paper. Per-dataset gaps to Table 8 > 0.03 are investigated (the split protocol differs, D2). |

## 6. Hyper-parameters

- **Stage `main` (defaults).**
  - Neural arms: official IMN defaults (§1). The TM arms also get the D15 regulariser chosen in Phase 1.
  - `tm`: the §3 defaults.
  - Paper Appendix B shows that the IMN conclusions hold with defaults, so this stage alone answers Q1–Q6
    in their "default" form.
- **Stage `hpo` (equal budget).**
  - 30 random-search trials per (arm, dataset), seeded (D11), selected on the validation AUROC of
    seed 0. The winner is re-run on seeds 0–4 and reported.
  - Search spaces:

  | Arms | Space |
  |---|---|
  | `imn`, `tabresnet`, `imn_tm*`, `imn_hybrid`, `tm_gated`, `clause_lr` | Paper Table 11: epochs [10, 500], lr [1e-5, 1e-1] log, batch {32 … 512}, weight decay [1e-5, 1e-1] log, dropout [0, 0.5]. The interpretable arms also search the L1 weight over [1e-5, 1] log ([1e-5, 10] for the clause arms, D19). The paper's range is [1e-5, 1e-1]; it is widened for every arm alike because the mean-normalised penalty scales with 1/P (D15). The clause arms also search `l1_mode ∈ {all, active, prior}`. |
  | `tm` (and the source TM of the clause arms) | clauses per class {50, 100, 200, 400, 800}, `T/clauses` {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2}, s {2, 3, 5, 8, 12, 20}, thermometer bits {5, 10, 20}, `max_included_literals` {None, 8, 16, 32} |
  | `lr`, `dt`, `rf`, `gbdt` | Paper Tables 12–15 (CatBoost space if CatBoost is used) |

  - For the clause arms, the source TM is fixed to the `tm` arm's HPO winner *before* their own search.
    This is two-stage, as in deployment, and it keeps the clause basis identical across `tm`,
    `clause_lr`, `imn_tm`, `imn_hybrid` and `tm_gated`.

## 7. Interpretability evaluation

The same protocol is used for every arm. Code is in `code/explain.py` and `code/xai.py`. It runs on a 500-example
test sub-sample of each record.

- **Attribution (paper Eq. 2, generalised):**
  - The attribution is `φ_m(x) = z(x; w(x)) − z(x with x_m := 0; w(x))`, with the local model frozen.
  - It equals `w_m x_m` for IMN (tested).
  - For clause local models, the clauses are re-evaluated on the modified input.
  - Arms without a local model use the same removal on the full model (occlusion).
- **Explanation size:**
  - Number of terms that cover 90 % of the |contribution| mass (`linear_terms_90`, `clause_terms_90`).
  - Number of active clauses and their mean length (literals a user must read).
- **Local fidelity (paper Table 1):** accuracy of the local model of `x_n` on its k ∈ {10, 25, 50} nearest
  test neighbours per class.
- **Self-consistency:** correlation between the frozen-local-model attribution and the removal effect on
  the full model (the hypernetwork re-run). It is 1 when the local model fully describes the model.
- **Synthetic ground truth (stage `xai`, Phase 4):**
  - Tasks: Gaussian Linear, Non-Linear Additive and Piecewise Constant, each with ρ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75}
    (`--dataset xai:linear_rho0.5`), d = 8, n = 10,000, seeds 0–4.
  - Metrics: `gt_corr` (exact Shapley correlation), faithfulness, infidelity (D10).
- **Global importance (paper Hypothesis 4):**
  - On adult, compute mean |φ| per feature (stored in every record).
  - Then zero the top-k features and report the AUROC drop per k (Figure 6 analogue; inference-time
    removal, no retraining).

## 8. Phases and gates

**Cost (pilot, 2026-10-14, RTX 3090/3080):**
- Training time:
  - Every 500-epoch neural arm takes about 10–15 s on the ≤ 1k-row datasets.
  - On adult (29k training rows) the same run takes about 130 s per 100 epochs, so ≈ 11 min.
  - The source TM takes 1–40 s.
- Main-stage estimate:
  - Adult and jungle-chess are about 6 GPU-h each (7 neural arms × 5 seeds).
  - The other 10 Tier-1 datasets add about 9 GPU-h, for a total of about 20 GPU-h sequential.
- GPU utilisation is only about 15 % (each step is a launch-bound small MLP). Running 3 processes per GPU on both
  GPUs should give about 4 h of wall time. This is re-estimated after Phase 1.

     --arm imn_tm imn_hybrid --seed 0 1 2 --nn "{\"l1_mode\": \"$m\", \"weight_norm\": $l}" --tag ${m}_${l}

## 10. Risks and how they are handled

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| `imn_tm` over-fits small data (seen in the pilot on credit-g) | D15 regulariser variants. Phase 1 selects on validation. The HPO searches dropout and L1. |
| A clause basis with hundreds of active clauses gives an unreadable explanation | `clause_terms_90` is reported. Ablation over source-TM size. `max_included_literals` in the TM HPO. |
| Comparison confounded by the source TM's quality | All clause arms share the cached TM. `clause_lr` and `imn_tm_rand` isolate the generator and the clauses. |
| Split protocol differs from the paper | Shown as a reference column only (D2). Our own `imn` vs `tabresnet` is the reproduction check. |
| Wide Tier-2 data (4–7k features) makes the TM slow | Tier 2 is optional and gets a cost estimate first. |
| Removal attribution for clause models depends on the baseline 0 (= train mean) | Same baseline for every arm, as in the paper. The ρ > 0 synthetic tasks show the correlation effects. |

## 11. Future work (not in this plan)

- Joint training: alternate TM feedback with hypernetwork updates, for example by feeding the
  hypernetwork's per-example clause weights back as feedback probabilities.
- Construction D with neighbourhood-constrained generated rules.
- Regression (IMN supports it; a `RegressionTsetlinMachine` source TM would be needed).
- Convolutional TM clauses with an IMN generator for the paper's image appendix.
